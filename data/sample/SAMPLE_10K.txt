Item 1. Business

Nimbus Systems designs and operates cloud infrastructure software for enterprise
customers. The company reports three operating segments: Cloud Platform, which
provides managed compute and storage services; Data Intelligence, which offers
analytics and machine-learning tooling; and Professional Services, which delivers
implementation and support engagements. The Cloud Platform segment is the largest
contributor to revenue and has grown as customers migrate workloads from on-premise
data centers to hosted environments. The company sells primarily through a direct
enterprise sales force, supplemented by a partner channel of resellers and systems
integrators. Nimbus serves customers across financial services, healthcare,
manufacturing, and the public sector, and no single customer represented more than
ten percent of total revenue in the most recent fiscal year.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

The company faces a number of risks that could materially affect its business,
financial condition, and results of operations. Competition in the cloud
infrastructure market is intense, and several larger competitors have greater
financial and engineering resources. A failure to keep pace with rapid technological
change could erode the company's market position. The business depends on the
reliability and security of its data centers; a significant cybersecurity incident,
data breach, or prolonged service outage could damage the company's reputation and
expose it to liability. The company also depends on third-party suppliers for
hardware components, and supply chain disruptions could increase costs or delay
capacity expansion. A substantial portion of revenue is recognized ratably over the
life of subscription contracts, so a slowdown in new bookings may not be reflected in
reported revenue until subsequent periods. Changes in data privacy regulation across
the jurisdictions in which the company operates could increase compliance costs and
restrict certain product features.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Total revenue increased during the fiscal year, driven primarily by expansion within
existing Cloud Platform customers and by growth in the Data Intelligence segment.
Net revenue retention remained above one hundred ten percent, reflecting the
company's ability to sell additional capacity and modules to its installed base.
Gross margin improved as the company realized economies of scale in data center
operations and shifted a larger share of workloads onto its own infrastructure.
Operating expenses grew in absolute terms as the company continued to invest in
research and development and in expanding its enterprise sales force, though operating
expenses declined as a percentage of revenue. The company generated positive free
cash flow for the year and ended the period with a strong liquidity position,
consisting of cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments sufficient to fund
planned capital expenditures on data center capacity. Management believes existing
liquidity and cash generated from operations will be sufficient to meet working
capital and capital expenditure requirements for at least the next twelve months.
